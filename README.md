<h1>E-Scooter Conversion</h1>

I started this because converting a Razor seemed like a neat thing to try, and then it turned out to have more interesting problems in it than I expected.

The rear wheel was the crux. There's no clean way to get drive torque into a stock Razor wheel, so it had to be replaced with something I could key to an axle — which meant printing a wheel, printing the tire separately in TPU, and epoxying the two together. Everything else followed from that: the new axle needed cross-drilling for set screws, the sprocket needed a printed mount and a shaft flange, and the motor's final position ended up being decided by chain geometry rather than by me.

None of the individual steps are difficult. Working out the order they have to happen in, and which dimensions depend on which, was most of the work.

So this is the approach I landed on, written down while it's still fresh.

Converting a Razor A5 Lux kick scooter into a chain-driven electric scooter using a 24 V brushed DC motor, an off-the-shelf motor controller, and two 3D-printed parts.
